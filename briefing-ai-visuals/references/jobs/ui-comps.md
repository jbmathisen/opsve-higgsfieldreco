# Job: website or app design comps (UI/UX)

Generated screens are for choosing a direction quickly and cheaply, before anyone writes code. They are references, not deliverables: the shipped page is built in code from them, with only plates (photos, materials, illustrations) carried over as rasters. Read `../taste.md` §2 and §3 first.

## Ready check

- [ ] The surface and its mode: landing page (Persuade), app screen or dashboard (Operate), docs (Read), showcase (Experience)
- [ ] Who arrives, in what situation, and the one thing they must understand or do
- [ ] Real content: product name, headline, the offer or the data, numbers with units, the next action by name; realistic minimum and maximum (long names, empty states)
- [ ] Sections in the order of the argument (not the stock hero, logos, features, testimonial, pricing, CTA by habit)
- [ ] An existing world or a new one: if the product already has a site or app, a screenshot of a representative page as the style reference
- [ ] Device and viewport: desktop landscape, or phone portrait at device size for mobile-first and app screens
- [ ] What must stay untouched, and what would feel wrong even if polished
- [ ] Who builds from the comps (a developer, Higgsfield's website skill, a coding agent with a design skill)

## Think

1. **Mode sets ambition.** On Persuade, bold and distinct; on Operate, ambition shows as precision: quiet panels, aligned figures, color spent on data, patterns the user already knows.
2. **One image per section,** horizontal, never a whole page in one tall image (detail turns to mush and variety dies). A landing page is often six sections, a full site eight.
3. **Commit to one combination before prompting** and hold it across all sections: theme (light, dark, a bold solid field, quiet neutral), type character, hero scale, section system, one narrative spine (for example archive, tool, journey, stage), and one second-read moment placed once. Write it into the brief.
4. **Vary per section:** what anchors the composition and the background mode; at least three different anchors across the page; never the same layout family three sections in a row.
5. **On an established brand,** the screenshot carries palette, type and component character; say in one sentence what it contributes and that its content and layout must not be copied. Describing the style in words as well overrides the picture.

## Make

1. **Comp prompt shape** (150 to 300 words, see `../templates.md`): the kind of screen and product by name, rendered edge to edge with crisp readable text and no browser or device around it; the visitor's job with the real content; what leads this comp; the parts in reading order, a phrase each; the frame (mark, navigation, utility controls). Leave sizes, positions and component styling out unless pinned.
2. **Three comps of the opening screen** that differ in structure, together, at one approval point. When the user picks, record what carries forward and what must not be copied literally.
3. **Then the remaining sections,** one image each, with the approved hero comp as the style reference.
4. **Self-check every render:** could it hang as a poster, or is it a photo with some text on it? Then it is not a comp; regenerate with the visitor's job as the prompt's spine.
5. **Hand over:** the approved comps, the combination, the palette hex, the type choices, and a list of which regions are plates (to regenerate at asset resolution) and which are code.

Higgsfield routes: GPT Image 2.5 is Higgsfield's default for UI, layouts and on-image text; Nano Banana Pro for reference-driven comps. Higgsfield's website skill generates one board per section and builds the site from them; Supercomputer can build landing pages from a brief. For a coding agent building from the comps, design skills such as Impeccable or Taste Skill pick up from here.

## Keeper checks (examples)

- The visitor's job and the next action are readable from the comp alone
- The mode can be named without a caption
- No tell from the dated list (especially fake dashboards, identical card trios, eyebrow labels)

## Typical spend

Stills only: three opening comps plus one per remaining section, with up to two re-rolls per build. A comp that fails twice means change the combination, not the wording.
